## Audience Understanding

- What the audience wants: Study less and remember more. A method that works the
  night before the exam. Essays that get done faster and still sound like them.
  Proof that other people feel the same way about school.
- What the audience struggles with: Rereading notes and forgetting everything.
  Starting essays. Turning an AI draft into something they can actually submit.
  Getting flagged by Turnitin or GPTZero, sometimes on writing they did
  themselves. Procrastination dressed up as "organizing."
- What they already believe: Everyone uses ChatGPT for school. Detectors are
  unfair and inconsistent. Highlighting and rereading are not real studying,
  even if they still do it. Aesthetic study setups are motivating.
- What they are tired of hearing: "Just start earlier." "Don't use AI." "Make a
  study schedule." Generic productivity advice with no method behind it.
  Influencers with Ivy credentials telling them to wake up at 5 a.m.
- What would make them save/share/comment: Usable techniques for stronger
  essays, clearer arguments, better revision, and more effective studying.
  Make the benefit visible in the headline and deliver the promised advice.
- What would make the account feel fake: Guru tone. Fake credentials.
  Motivational quotes. Selling in the hook line. Claiming a method works for
  everyone. Stock-photo studying. Overpromising on detectors ("100%
  undetectable").

## Account Point Of View

- Core belief: Study smarter with systems, not longer with willpower. Using AI
  for school is normal; sounding like a bot is the problem.
- Enemy or tension: Wasted effort. Rereading, passive highlighting, generic
  advice, and robotic AI drafts that get flagged.
- Promise: Give students practical techniques they can use on their next
  assignment. Keep product mentions in the relevant closing CTA.
- Emotional tone: Direct, useful, concise, peer-to-peer. A student sharing
  study tips with a clear academic payoff.
- What the account should never say: "Grind." "Lock in" as advice. "Just
  believe in yourself." "Guaranteed A." "Never get caught." Anything that
  frames students as cheaters or the account as anti-education.
