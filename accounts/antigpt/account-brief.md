# Account Brief

## Account Basics

- Account name: antigpt (Instagram: https://www.instagram.com/antigpt.me/)
- Niche: Study habits and academic productivity. Memorization techniques,
  writing techniques, note-taking systems, focus routines, exam-week plans.
  Study-aesthetic visuals.
- Target audience: High school and college students who study with a laptop and
  AI tools open, care about grades and how their study setup looks, and save
  tips for later. Secondary: grad students writing long papers.
- Platform priority: Instagram first (confirmed channel), TikTok second.
- Primary performance goal: Saves and follows first; profile visits and clicks
  to antigpt.me second. The account exists to promote AntiGPT, but only the
  last slide of each post does the promoting.
- Product relationship: Direct promotion of AntiGPT (antigpt.me) on the last
  slide of every post. See `../../product/antigpt-brief.md` and
  `../../product/antigpt-claim-bank.md`. This account has no relationship to
  w(inner).

## Audience Understanding

- What the audience wants: Study less and remember more. A method that works the
  night before the exam. Essays that get done faster and still sound like them.
  A study setup that looks as good as it feels.
- What the audience struggles with: Rereading notes and forgetting everything.
  Starting essays. Turning an AI draft into something they can actually submit.
  Getting flagged by Turnitin or GPTZero, sometimes on writing they did
  themselves. Procrastination dressed up as "organizing."
- What they already believe: Everyone uses ChatGPT for school. Detectors are
  unfair and inconsistent. Highlighting and rereading are not real studying,
  even if they still do it. Aesthetic notes are motivating.
- What they are tired of hearing: "Just start earlier." "Don't use AI." "Make a
  study schedule." Generic productivity advice with no method behind it.
  Influencers with Ivy credentials telling them to wake up at 5 a.m.
- What would make them save/share: A named technique with steps they can try
  tonight (active recall, blurting, Feynman, spaced repetition, Cornell notes).
  A ranking or comparison that settles which method is worth their time. An
  essay-writing sequence they can follow slide by slide. Anything that
  shortens the time between "I have to study" and "I am done."
- What would make the account feel fake: Guru tone. Fake credentials.
  Motivational quotes. Selling on slide 1. Claiming a method works for
  everyone. Stock-photo studying. Overpromising on detectors ("100%
  undetectable").

## Account Point Of View

- Core belief: Study smarter with systems, not longer with willpower. Using AI
  for school is normal; sounding like a bot is the problem.
- Enemy or tension: Wasted effort. Rereading, passive highlighting, generic
  advice, and robotic AI drafts that get flagged.
- Promise: Every post gives one study method you can use today. The last slide
  gives you the tool that makes AI-assisted writing sound human and bypass
  Turnitin.
- Emotional tone: Calm, specific, slightly dry, peer-to-peer. A smart classmate
  sharing what worked, not a coach. Study-aesthetic warmth in visuals, plain
  language in copy.
- What the account should never say: "Grind." "Lock in" as advice. "Just
  believe in yourself." "Guaranteed A." "Never get caught." Anything that
  frames students as cheaters or the account as anti-education.

## Angle Variant Preferences

- Strongest desire levers: Remember more in less time. Finish the essay
  tonight. Stop getting flagged. Look organized and feel in control.
- Weak angle patterns to avoid: Pure motivation. "Day in my life" without a
  method. Generic "5 study tips" with no mechanism. Product-first hooks.
- Identity gaps that resonate: "I study for hours and still forget" vs "I
  study less and remember it." "My essay sounds like a robot" vs "it sounds
  like me."
- Viral curiosity patterns that fit the account: Rankings ("ranked by how much
  they actually helped"), matchers ("the right method for each subject"),
  mechanism reveals ("why rereading feels productive and does nothing"),
  sequences ("the order I write an essay in"), mistake-and-fix lists.
- Example winning variant: Pending A1.

## Product Mention Decision

- Default product visibility: Last slide only. Every post ends with an AntiGPT
  slide.
- Best native placement: Final slide as the natural last step of the method
  ("you drafted it, now make it sound like you and bypass Turnitin"). Writing
  and essay posts bridge most naturally; memorization posts use a standalone
  closing CTA.
- Placements to avoid: Slide 1. Mid-post mentions. Multiple product slides.
  Detector claims stacked on one slide.
- Claims approved: See `../../product/antigpt-claim-bank.md`. Direct "bypass
  Turnitin / bypass AI detectors" wording is approved by the user.
- Claims flagged: "100% undetectable", pass-rate percentages, user counts,
  named-school testimonials, free-trial framing (it requires a card).

## Research Decisions

| Content pillar | Claim risk | Source type needed | User notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Memorization techniques (active recall, spaced repetition, Feynman, blurting, interleaving) | Low. Well-documented learning science. | Named technique + one-line mechanism; cite a study only if quoting a number | Keep numbers out unless sourced |
| Writing techniques (thesis, outlining, paragraph structure, editing, voice) | Low | Standard composition guidance | Natural bridge to the AntiGPT CTA |
| Note-taking systems (Cornell, mind maps, digital vs paper) | Low | Method descriptions | Strong study-aesthetic visuals |
| Focus and routines (Pomodoro, environment, phone rules) | Low | Method descriptions | Avoid 5 a.m. guru framing |
| AI for schoolwork without sounding robotic | Medium. Detector and integrity optics. | Product brief + claim bank | Bypass wording approved; keep it to the last slide |
| Detector claims (Turnitin, GPTZero) | Medium | Site copy only; no invented pass rates | Never absolute |
